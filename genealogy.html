Book of Ruth — The Moabitess

A Moabite widow whose loyalty to Naomi led her to Bethlehem. Her kinsman-redeemer Boaz married her. A Gentile grandmother of David, demonstrating God's love for the nations.

BATHSHEBA

2 Samuel 11-12 — Wife of Uriah

Taken by David in adultery, yet through her Solomon was born. Matthew deliberately avoids naming her, instead emphasizing Uriah — a perpetual reminder of David's sin and God's forgiveness.

The climax of the women in the line. A virgin of Nazareth who conceived by the Holy Spirit. Through her "yes," God became flesh. Her lineage through Nathan (Luke 3) provides the biological descent of Jesus, complementing Joseph's legal line through Solomon.

ERA VIII THE SOLOMONIC DYNASTY — ETHIOPIA "The kings of Ethiopia shall bear the title 'Lion of the Tribe of Judah.'" — Kebra Nagast
After the resurrection, the Davidic covenant extended beyond Judah into Ethiopia. Per Ethiopian tradition, the Solomonic dynasty ruled for nearly 3,000 years — the longest continuous royal line in history — until 1974. 📖 This branch links the Ancestral Scroll to the Tewahedo Archive

MENELIK I

c. 10th Century BC — Ebna La-Hakim

Son of King Solomon of Israel and the Queen of Sheba (Makeda). According to the Kebra Nagast, Menelik journeyed to Jerusalem as a young man, was recognized by Solomon, and later returned to Ethiopia bearing the Ark of the Covenant. He founded the Solomonic dynasty that would rule Ethiopia for three millennia.

KEBRA NAGAST — Chapters 29–64
📖 Cross-reference: 1 Kings 10:1-13 (Queen of Sheba), 📖 Tewahedo Archive
📜 Kebra Nagast
מְנֶלֶךׁ / ኪኛአቬቝ

EZANA OF AXUM

c. 320–360 AD — Emperor of Axum

The first Christian emperor of the Axumite Kingdom, converted under the teaching of Frumentius (Abba Selama). Ezana made Christianity the state religion of Ethiopia — one of the first nations to do so. His trilingual inscriptions (Ge'ez, Sabaean, Greek) parallel the multicultural character of the Solomonic covenant reaching the nations.

EZANA INSCRIPTIONS — Monumentum Adulitanum, 4th Century AD
📖 Cross-reference: Rufinus, Church History 10.9–10
🏛 Axum Obelisks
אֶזָנָה / ᓔᓊኛኋ

GEBRE MESKEL

c. 6th Century AD — Emperor of Axum

Son of Emperor Kaleb. According to tradition, Gebre Meskel ("Servant of the Cross") built several of Ethiopia's most ancient churches. His reign saw the consolidation of Christianity in the highlands and the translation of Scripture into Ge'ez — the language that would preserve uniquely Ethiopian biblical traditions.

ETHIOPIAN ORTHODOX TRADITION — 6th Century AD
📖 Cross-reference: Tewahedo Archive — Ge'ez biblical manuscripts

LALIBELA

c. 1181–1221 AD — Emperor of the Zagwe Dynasty

Emperor of the Zagwe dynasty, traditionally recognized as a Solomonic heir through a female line. Lalibela is renowned for commissioning the eleven rock-hewn churches of Lalibela — a "New Jerusalem" carved from living stone. These churches symbolize the earthly Jerusalem and prefigure the heavenly, continuing the typological thread that began in the Tabernacle.

LALIBELA CHURCHES — UNESCO World Heritage Site, 12th–13th Century AD
📖 Cross-reference: Typology — The earthly sanctuary as a pattern of the heavenly
🏛 Rock-Hewn Churches

YEKUNO AMLAK

c. 1270–1285 AD — Emperor of Ethiopia

Claiming direct descent from Solomon through the male line, Yekuno Amlak overthrew the Zagwe dynasty and restored the Solomonic dynasty to the Ethiopian throne. His reign marked the beginning of the Solomonic Restoration, a period in which the Kebra Nagast was likely compiled to legitimize and celebrate the dynasty's divine mandate.

SOLOMONIC RESTORATION — 1270 AD
📖 Cross-reference: Kebra Nagast, Ethiopian Royal Chronicles
📜 Kebra Nagast

ZARA YACOB

c. 1434–1468 AD — Emperor of Ethiopia

One of Ethiopia's greatest emperors. Zara Yacob ("Seed of Jacob") was a religious reformer who centralized the Church, translated Scripture into Ge'ez, and elevated the veneration of the Ark of the Covenant. He composed theological works including the Matshafa Berhan (Book of Light), defending the Orthodox faith against both Islamic and Latin influences.

ETHIOPIAN RENAISSANCE — 15th Century AD
📖 Cross-reference: Tewahedo Archive — Ge'ez manuscripts of his reign
📜 Book of Light

FASILIDES

c. 1632–1667 AD — Emperor of Ethiopia

Emperor who established Gondar as the capital of Ethiopia and built its famous castle complex. Fasilides expelled the Jesuits and restored Orthodox Christianity as the sole state religion. The Fasil Ghebbi castle-palace complex stands as a symbol of Solomonic imperial power, with each subsequent emperor adding to the royal compound.

GONDARINE PERIOD — 17th Century AD
📖 Cross-reference: Fasil Ghebbi, UNESCO World Heritage Site

TEWODROS II

c. 1855–1868 AD — Emperor of Ethiopia

The last Solomonic emperor of the medieval direct line. Tewodros II sought to reunify and modernize Ethiopia. His tragic end — taking his own life at the Battle of Magdala rather than being captured by British forces — marked the end of an era. Yet the Solomonic claim continued through a lateral branch, sustaining the dynastic identity.

MODERNIZATION ERA — 19th Century AD
📖 Cross-reference: British Museum — Magdala Treasures

MENELIK II

c. 1889–1913 AD — Emperor of Ethiopia

Proclaimed himself of Solomonic descent through a lateral line. Menelik II is celebrated for the Battle of Adwa (1896), where Ethiopian forces decisively defeated Italian colonialism — the first African victory over a European colonial power. He modernized Ethiopia, established Addis Ababa as the capital, and introduced the first telegraph, railway, and postal system.

BATTLE OF ADWA — 1 March 1896
📖 Cross-reference: 📖 See Adwa on the Navigator

HAILE SELASSIE I

c. 1930–1974 AD — The Last Solomonic Emperor

The 225th and final Emperor of the Solomonic dynasty. Haile Selassie I ("Power of the Trinity") was a constitutional monarch, a founding figure of the Organization of African Unity, and a symbol of African independence. His full imperial title — "Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah, King of Kings of Ethiopia, Elect of God" — explicitly invoked the Davidic covenant. His overthrow in 1974 ended the world's longest continuously reigning dynasty.

SOLOMONIC DYNASTY — c. 950 BC – 1974 AD
📖 Cross-reference: Kebra Nagast, Ethiopian Constitution of 1931, OAU Charter 1963
